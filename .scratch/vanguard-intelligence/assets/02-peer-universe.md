Business-mix and revenue-model notes beyond what a page showed this session are tagged ⚠️ UNVERIFIED — the code review of ticket 01 established that untagged inferences are the failure mode to avoid.

**Comparability frame (from the ticket):** Vanguard is a client-owned (mutual) asset manager with no firm-level statements. A peer is *data-comparable* when it publishes audited firm financials (10-K or equivalent) for the 5-year window; it is *business-comparable* when its mix (index vs. active, retail vs. institutional, fee-based AUM) overlaps Vanguard's. Ownership itself is the headline caveat: mutual (Vanguard) vs. listed (most) vs. private (Fidelity) — RoE comparisons must carry it (spec stories 12, 14; map note).

## 2. Comparability analysis (feeds ticket 04 peer-set decision)

> Analysis below restates the tagged rows from §1; statements here inherit the verification status of the row they derive from (⚠️ unless the underlying cell was ✅). Items 4–6 go slightly beyond the ticket's three axes (ownership, product mix, revenue model) — kept as leads for the fact base (ticket 08), not as peer-set criteria.

1. **Ownership axis — three regimes.** Mutual (Vanguard), listed (BlackRock, State Street, T. Rowe Price, Franklin, Invesco, Amundi, Janus Henderson, Northern Trust), private (Fidelity — and ⚠️ UNVERIFIED leads for the "and others" in the ticket: Capital Group, Wellington, Dimensional, and the private arms of bank/insurer groups; none have audited firm statements published, so they fall in the same data-limited bucket as Fidelity). Listed peers have audited statements and thus computable RoE; private Fidelity does not, so *Fidelity can only be compared on metrics it volunteers* (AUM, clients, qualitative mix) — and any such comparison inherits ticket 01's RoE-proxy constraints. A peer set that includes Fidelity must state which metrics are comparable per firm.
2. **Business mix axis.** Vanguard's mix (index-heavy funds/ETFs + retirement + brokerage + advice + institutional) overlaps most with: BlackRock (scale + index/ETF + iShares vs. Vanguard ETFs), State Street/SSGA (index/ETF, but servicing-led parent), Invesco (index/ETF + active + QQQ), T. Rowe Price (retirement + active, no index), Fidelity (retail breadth, index + active + brokerage + advice). Pure-active peers (T. Rowe Price, Janus Henderson) are useful *contrast* cases for cost-ratio and revenue-intensity comparisons but mislead on product-mix parity.
3. **Revenue model axis.** Fee-based AUM is the shared model, but: BlackRock adds Aladdin technology fees; State Street and Northern Trust add custody/servicing fees and net interest income; Amundi adds bancassurance distribution economics; Fidelity adds brokerage/advice/recordkeeping revenue. Revenue *totals* are not like-for-like across these; revenue *per AUM dollar* (fee rate) or expense-ratio comparison is the safer plane.
4. **Reporting calendars.** Most FYE Dec 31; **Franklin Sep 30** and **Janus Henderson Jun 30** are staggered — the fact base must align to fiscal-year labels or carry as-of dates (spec story 3/4: definitions and year coverage per metric).
5. **GAAP regime.** US candidates file US GAAP 10-K; **Amundi reports IFRS in EUR** via URD. Currency and accounting-standard differences need explicit handling in any cross-border comparison.
6. **Scale spread.** AUM spans ~$1.9T (T. Rowe Price) to ~$10T+ (BlackRock ⚠️) plus Vanguard's unpublished firm AUM (ticket 01: only through Mar 31, 2022; FY2022–25 in Form ADV PDF). Absolute-scale comparisons are fine; per-dollar metrics are the meaningful plane.

**Bottom line for ticket 02:** The candidate universe splits cleanly into *listed, fully data-comparable* firms (BlackRock, State Street, T. Rowe Price, Franklin, Invesco, Amundi, Janus Henderson, Northern Trust — all file audited statements), one *private, data-limited* firm (Fidelity — voluntary stats only), and the *mutual subject* (Vanguard — headline stats only). Data comparability is not the same as business comparability: BlackRock, Fidelity, and (partially) Invesco and SSGA are the closest business comparators to Vanguard's index/ETF + retirement + advice mix; the pure-active firms and the bank-parented firms are useful for specific metric contrasts but need segment isolation or mix caveats. Ownership, revenue-model, fiscal-calendar, and GAAP differences all need explicit handling in the peer set decision (ticket 04) and the fact base (ticket 08).
